What this is

A planner for Tesla's 2026 Free Supercharging Competition, "Longest Trip" category: the longest continuous streak of unique Supercharger sites (a distinct location as shown in the app / navigation) where you begin a charging session at each new site within 24 hours of the previous session — Tesla's page says "start time" in the category definition and "end of your previous session" in the trip explanation, so plan with the stricter start-anchored timer (Settings lets you switch). Repeat visits are allowed but neither count nor reset the timer. Sessions anywhere in the world count; you compete in the region where you visited the most unique sites. Ties break on total kWh. Competition period: 1 Jan 2026 00:01 – 31 Dec 2026 23:59 local time.

Don't forget to enroll: enable "Share Charging Data with Tesla App" in the vehicle settings, open the 2026 Passport in the Tesla app before 1 Jan 2027 (enrollment opens December 2026), and open it again after your last Supercharger session so every session is counted. Vehicles that already have free Supercharging and commercial-use vehicles are not eligible.

Instead of the fewest stops, this planner helps you chain the most stops: short hops, small charges, never below your reserve, and never past the deadline.

How to use

  1. Set the start (search a place, click the map, or pick a charger), the departure time and battery level.
  2. Optionally set a destination so the "Next stop" list only shows sites that make progress.
  3. Add the next charger from the Next stop list or by clicking a red dot on the map. The app routes the real road (ferries included), samples elevation and weather, and predicts the arrival battery.
  4. Set how much to charge at each stop (the card shows the minimum useful level for the next hop) and add rests (sleep) with or without Sentry mode.
  5. Watch the streak timer on every card and in the header.
  6. Auto-chain repeatedly adds the nearest new site that makes road-distance progress toward the destination, charging just enough at each stop (it raises the previous charge target only when the next hop needs more). If a stop you added is too far (arrives below reserve), its card shows ⛽ Insert stops before — the planner fills that gap the same way, preferring sites you have not visited this year.
  7. The destination is routed and timed as the final leg (the "D" card at the end); ↩ roundtrip sets it back to your start so the return is part of the plan — and because suggestions prefer unvisited sites, the way back uses different chargers.
  8. “+NN km toward” on a suggestion is the road-distance progress toward the destination that the hop gains (“(line)” = straight-line estimate when the routing table was unavailable). 📍 pans the map to that charger; the popup of a planned charger also offers Remove stop #N. The Itinerary button (top bar) lays the whole plan out as a calendar — one column per day, driving / charging / rests on an hour grid, with the red ⏱ streak-deadline line.
  9. Every stop card has a battery bar — solid = the level you arrive with, blue = what you charge there (amber = what you lose while resting), the white tick = your reserve. The start (S), destination (D) and numbered badges on the map carry the same bar, and repeat visits to one site fan out side by side with an amber pass number. The map filter (All / ⚡ Reachable / 🏅 Iconic) limits the dots to what the car can reach right now from the end of the plan, or to badge sites — your own stops, start, destination and route always stay visible. ⇄ on a card starts a replace: pick another charger from the map or the list and it swaps in, keeping the charge level and rest.
  10. Export the plan as JSON (everything included: settings, car, stops, cached routes) and Import it later; the browser also autosaves.

Model notes

  • Consumption: aerodynamic + rolling + climbing physics with regen, heat-pump HVAC by temperature, wind along the route direction, wet-road factor, a cold-battery factor and a configurable safety margin (the in-car estimate is usually optimistic).
  • This is the same kind of physics the car's own trip planner runs, plus a driving-dynamics term (accelerations, curves, villages) that steady-state models miss. It is calibrated to land a few percent above the Tesla app's prediction — which itself tends to arrive ~2 % optimistic — so the plan errs on the safe side. Compare a few real legs and tune the margin, rolling resistance and payload in Settings until it matches your car.
  • Speeds come from OpenStreetMap limits via OSRM plus your driving profile (e.g. +5 km/h on roads ≥ 60 km/h).
  • Charging: the Model Y LR curve capped by the site power (V2 = 150 kW), 0.5 % integration steps, plug-in overhead, 6 % charging losses for the kWh counter.
  • Parked drain: Sentry ≈ 0.2 %/h (≈ 5 %/day on 2024.38+ firmware), off ≈ 0.04 %/h, ×1.3 below 0 °C.
  • Ferries: no consumption during the crossing; crossing time = 8 min docking + distance at 22 km/h (OSRM's own ferry times run slow) plus a configurable wait. Note: the OSRM demo profile skips some long car ferries (e.g. Moskenes–Bodø).
  • Visited earlier this year (map popup) feeds the yearly unique-sites counter and pushes those sites down the suggestion list — a streak may still include them, they just add nothing new for the year. Sites marked 🏅 are Iconic Charger badge locations (community list in data/iconic.json — extend it as Tesla reveals more badges).
  • Map colors: red dot = open Supercharger · grey = temporarily closed · hollow red = planned / under construction · amber = visited earlier this year · green = in your current trip (the numbered badge with its battery sits on top) · blue ring = suggested next stop · 🏅 = Iconic badge site.

Iconic Charger badges

Tesla's 40 location badges (Tesla app → Charging Passport → Iconic Chargers). Sites with ✓ carry a 🏅 pin on the map; "best guess" rows are community-identified — edit data/iconic.json to correct them.

Data & services

Sites: supercharge.info (community database, refresh with tools/fetch_chargers.py). Routing: OSRM demo server (fair use, self-host for heavy use). Weather: Open-Meteo. Elevation: Valhalla (FOSSGIS OSM demo), Open-Meteo as fallback. Search: Photon / Nominatim. Map: OpenStreetMap / CARTO.
